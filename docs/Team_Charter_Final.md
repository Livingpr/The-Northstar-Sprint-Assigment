# **1\. Client & Project Context**

Northstar Retail Co. is a mid-size e-commerce company whose support team is overloaded by repetitive questions about order status, returns/refunds, and stock availability. The team has one week to build an MVP that demonstrates a support-deflection approach rather than a finished production product.

Assessment requirement: the MVP must reduce manual ticket handling for at least two of the three ticket categories and be demoable end-to-end. The assessment also requires a one-page go-live readiness note and a collaborative commit/edit audit trail.

# **5\. Team Working Agreement**

* The 10 board tasks are the team's agreed units of work; no task may exceed four hours under the anti-black-box rule.  
* Each task has one accountable owner. Other members may collaborate, review, test, or provide support, but ownership remains visible.  
* The live board is updated on the same day that work occurs.  
* Every meaningful commit/edit follows: \<type\>: \<what changed\> \- \<why it matters\>.  
* Messages such as 'wip,' 'updates,' or 'changes' are not acceptable audit evidence.  
* Blockers are reported as soon as they occur and are not hidden until the deadline.  
* Evidence must be preserved as work happens; the team will not reconstruct an artificial history at the end.  
* The team prioritizes completion of the working MVP and assessment evidence over unnecessary feature expansion.  
* The confidential Peer Reliability Index is completed individually and is not shared verbatim between teammates.

# **6A. Decision-Making Process**

The team will use a clear, evidence-based decision process so that technical, scope, and deadline decisions are made quickly without creating unnecessary conflict.

| Decision type | Process | Final decision owner |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Routine implementation decision | The task owner may decide within the agreed scope and inform the team in the WhatsApp group. | Task owner |
| Technical decision affecting another task | Discuss with affected owner(s); agree on the option that best protects the MVP, timeline, and integration. | Relevant task owners: Project/product coordination resolves deadlock. |
| Scope / feature decision | Check whether the change is necessary for the MVP and whether it fits the agreed task scope and four-hour anti-black-box rule. | Project/Product Lead with team input |
| Priority / deadline decision | Review impact on delivery, assessment requirements, and dependencies; highest-risk/highest-value work takes precedence. | Project/Product Lead |
| Conflict or deadlock | Discuss facts and alternatives; each side gets a chance to explain its position; seek consensus first. If consensus cannot be reached, the designated lead makes the final call and records the decision. | Project/Product Lead |

# **6C. Ground Rules & Team Norms**

* Respect every team member's contribution and allow others to finish explaining their position before responding.  
* Keep communication professional, direct, and solution-focused; disagreements are about the work, not the person.  
* Respond to important WhatsApp messages and blocker requests in a reasonable time during agreed working hours.  
* Do not disappear when blocked. Notify the team early and state what help or decision is required.  
* Own assigned tasks from start to Definition of Done, while asking for help when necessary.  
* Keep the live board accurate on the same day the work happens.  
* Use the required commit/edit convention and avoid meaningless messages such as 'wip,' 'updates,' or 'changes.'  
* Do not claim work that another member completed; contribution must remain accurately attributable.  
* Do not create artificial or retrospective evidence. Audit records must reflect the actual work history.  
* Keep the end-of-day Google Meet recap focused and practical: completed work, blockers, decisions, and next steps.  
* Respect the confidentiality of the Peer Reliability Index; individual responses must not be shared verbatim between teammates.

# **6D. Conflict Resolution Process**

Conflicts are expected to be handled early and professionally. The goal is to resolve the issue without delaying the MVP or damaging the team's working relationship.

| Step | Action |
| :---- | :---- |
| 1\. Identify | The people involved clearly state the issue, the specific point of disagreement, and the effect on the task/project. |
| 2\. Discuss | The parties discuss the issue directly and respectfully, using requirements, evidence, deadlines, and task ownership rather than personal opinions. |
| 3\. Seek consensus | The team considers the available options and selects the solution that best protects the MVP requirements, quality, and deadline. |
| 4\. Escalate if unresolved | If the disagreement cannot be resolved, the Project/Product Lead facilitates a final discussion and makes the decision where necessary. |
| 5\. Record | If the conflict changes a task, priority, scope, dependency, or technical direction, the resulting decision is recorded in the appropriate project documentation/board and communicated to the team. |
| 6\. Recover | If the conflict caused delay or blocked work, the owner and team agree on a recovery action and update the board accordingly. |

# **8\. Definition of Done Standard**

# **12\. Anti-Black-Box & Change-Control Rule**

The assessment states that no board task may represent more than four hours of work. The team's ten agreed tasks therefore remain the official board units. If a task begins to exceed its estimate, the owner must immediately flag the issue rather than silently extending the task.

* First, identify why the task is expanding.  
* Second, notify the team and record the issue.  
* Third, if the work can no longer reasonably fit within the task's ≤4-hour scope, split the work into a clearly traceable additional unit only with team agreement and board documentation.  
* Never hide additional work inside an existing task or create a retrospective audit trail.
